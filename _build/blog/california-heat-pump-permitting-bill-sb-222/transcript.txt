[1:19] So the specific bills we're going to be talking about in this episode are California Senate Bill 222 and Senate Bill 282. So here are the main bullet points and objectives of Senate Bill 222. The first main one is to make heat pump installations easier and faster by requiring cities and counties in California to allow faster inspections. And this includes inspections where the contractor doesn't have to physically be there unless there's a problem. And by 2028, these same cities and counties must offer instant online permits for straightforward residential heat pump installs. It also requires these permit applications and associated documents to be available online and submittable electronically. The second big objective is to limit red tape and fees by preventing local governments from charging excessive permitting fees for heat pumps.
[2:09] And these permitting fees are generally capped at actual costs, which is just defined as whatever the labor time and administrative cost of actually administering the permit is. And there's a hard $150 limit on permits for water heaters. It also makes it so that plug-in window unit ACs or heat pumps that are 120 volts or less do not require any permits or inspections. Third big thing is that it stops HOAs and contracts from being able to block electrification. So HOAs, homeowners associations, we all know them and love them, make you bring in your trash can within 30 minutes or whatever. But there are a lot of HOAs that will block heat pumps from being installed because of aesthetics or noise concerns or other reasons.
[2:53] But this bill would prevent HOAs statewide from being able to prohibit installing heat pumps. And it actually prevents them from being able to prohibit replacing any gas appliance with an electric one. So I think that's pretty cool as well. Now, the fourth thing is that it does maintain some local control. So these cities and counties, they can still enforce building efficiency rules or local energy codes. And they can require separate permits for electric panel upgrades, demolition work, or when there's a significant amount of structural work that needs to be done to the home or building where the heat pump is being installed. And the way this is going to be enforced on the state level is that these local governments must confirm to follow these rules if they want certain energy-related funding from the state's energy agency, which is the California Energy Commission. So all in all, the main goal is to speed up permits, lower costs, and remove barriers so more homeowners make the switch from gas appliances over to heat pumps.
