Heat pumps in Arkansas

Arkansas runs at 19.7% of homes on a heat pump. It sits on the boundary where the Southern pattern starts to take over — enough cooling demand that most homes need the equipment anyway, and enough winter that how well it heats still matters.

Money and next steps

What Arkansas pays toward one

  • Arkansas Energy Office

    Part of the Arkansas Economic Development Commission, coordinating state energy programmes. Entergy Arkansas, Oklahoma Gas & Electric and the co-ops run the residential rebates.

    Program details →

  • Federal Home Energy Rebates

    The IRA rebate programs (HEAR and HOMES) survived the 2025 tax changes — they were grants to states, not tax credits. Each state runs its own, and they are at different stages: some open, some already out of money, some yet to launch. Check where Arkansas stands before you count on it.

    Program details →

The federal 25C tax credit — 30% back, up to $2,000 on a heat pump — ended for systems placed in service after 31 December 2025. If you had one installed in 2025 or earlier you can still claim it; there is no successor credit. Incentives move fast, so check DSIRE for what is current in Arkansas. Last checked September 2026.

Both loads matter here — cold snaps are real, and so are the summers. A system sized only for heating will short-cycle in August; one sized only for cooling will lean on backup heat in January. Getting the balance right is the job.
